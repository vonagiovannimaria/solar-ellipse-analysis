# Solar Ellipse — A Parametric-Esthesic Analysis

**La forma all'ascolto: Solar Ellipse di Barry Truax — Regioni e articolazioni interne in una prospettiva parametrico-estesica**

An analytical study of **Barry Truax's *Solar Ellipse* (1984–85)**, focusing on how audible changes in sonic parameters shape the perception of musical form.

This work was prepared by **Giovanni Maria Vona** for the course *Analisi della Musica Elettroacustica II* (Electroacoustic Music Analysis II), taught by **Prof. Marco Marinoni**, as part of the First-Level Academic Diploma in Sound Engineering (*Tecnico del Suono*) at the **Conservatorio Statale di Musica “O. Respighi”**, Latina, Italy (A.Y. 2025/2026).

## Overview

The study proposes a **parametric-esthesic approach** to the formal segmentation of *Solar Ellipse*. Its main research question is:

> Which audible changes make it possible to distinguish larger regions and internal articulations within a musical process characterized by recurring patterns?

The analysis examines five complementary listening dimensions:

1. **Perceived intensity** — dynamic levels, increases, decreases, and climaxes.
2. **Register and pitch** — prevailing register, identifiable pitch changes, and shifts of tonal emphasis.
3. **Timbre** — formant-like and noise-based qualities, brightness, and perceived spectral richness.
4. **Density and layering** — overlap, fusion, and the perceptual distinction between sound components.
5. **Temporal development** — waves, recurring gestures, local fluctuations, and larger trajectories.

The parameters are used as **operational listening criteria**, not as an automated feature-extraction model or a claim of objective, uniquely determined segmentation.

## Main Findings

The proposed segmentation identifies **four main regions** and **eighteen internal articulations** (4 + 6 + 4 + 4).

| Region | Time range | Predominant characteristics | Internal articulations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I — Presentation | 0:00–2:45 | Distinguishable waves, moderate layering, progressive intensification | 4 |
| II — Overlap | 2:45–5:49 | Greater intensity and superposition, with marked local contrasts | 6 |
| III — Renewed distinction | 5:49–8:02 | Reduced layering; wave-like processes become easier to distinguish | 4 |
| IV — Closure | 8:02–end | Attenuation and simplification, followed by a final dynamic build-up | 4 |

The three principal boundaries occur at approximately **2:45**, **5:49**, and **8:02**. Their significance is justified through combinations of audible cues rather than amplitude changes alone.

A central conclusion is that **perceived intensity, timbral complexity, and layering do not necessarily evolve together**. In particular, the final build-up grows dynamically without recreating the timbral complexity of the second region.

## Context and Method

*Solar Ellipse* was composed in 1984–85 and belongs to Truax's *Sequence of Earlier Heaven* cycle. The project draws on the composer's own descriptions of its frequency-modulation synthesis, layered cyclical structures, and track-selection processes.

The study carefully distinguishes **documented compositional procedures** from **formal articulations inferred through listening**: the four larger constructions described by Truax are not assumed to correspond directly to the four perceptual regions identified in this analysis.

Listening and temporal segmentation were carried out in **REAPER**, using markers and waveform views to locate, revisit, and compare passages. Waveform plots are used as temporal and amplitude references; they are **not** treated as stand-alone proof of timbre, loudness perception, or formal boundaries.

A short documentary comparison with Truax's earlier work *Wave Edge* (1983) further contextualizes the relationship between recurring local processes and overall musical design. This is not presented as a second full perceptual analysis.

### Recording and scope

The time references correspond to the stereo WAV supplied for the course (**44.1 kHz, 16-bit; approximately 10:57.59**). The original work was mixed for four-channel reproduction; consequently, the stereo version does not allow a complete analysis of its spatial organization.

The segmentation is an argued listening-based interpretation, not a claim that every listener will identify exactly the same boundaries.
